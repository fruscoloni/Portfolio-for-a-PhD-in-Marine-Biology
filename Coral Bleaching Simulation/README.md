# Coral Bleaching Analysis: Evaluating Thermal Stress via Linear Mixed-Effects Models (LMMs)

# Overview
As global sea surface temperatures rise, thermal stress events are becoming the primary driver of worldwide coral bleaching and mortality. Quantifying how different coral species respond to elevated temperatures across spatial and temporal scales is critical for marine conservation and reef resilience management.

This repository contains an end-to-end data science project written in R, showcasing a complete data workflow: from cleaning messy, multi-source raw monitoring datasets to fitting Linear Mixed-Effects Models (LMMs) and communicating ecological insights through publication-ready visualizations.

# Data Source
The analysis uses a synthetic marine monitoring dataset designed to simulate real-world field conditions:
* **Biological Monitoring Dataset (`Messy_coral.csv`)**: Contains repeated observations of bleaching percentage across individual coral colonies (`Colony_ID`), species, and sampling dates, featuring common data-entry errors.
* **Environmental Dataset (`Messy_env.csv`)**: Temperature records (°C) and depth categories across multiple reef sites (`Site`) formatted in a wide layout.

# Methodology
The entire pipeline—from raw data cleaning and geostatistical/spatial nesting to model diagnostics—was built in R.

**Key Libraries Used:**
* **Data Tidying & Wrangling**: `tidyverse` (`dplyr`, `tidyr`, `stringr`, `forcats`), `janitor`
* **Statistical Modeling & Extraction**: `lme4`, `lmerTest`, `broom.mixed`
* **Model Diagnostics**: `DHARMa` (simulated residual analysis)
* **Visualization**: `ggplot2`, `patchwork`

### Analytical Approach:
1. **Data Tidying & Cleaning**: Standardized species nomenclature using regex, cleaned numerical data containing mixed strings/symbols, and pivoted wide environmental parameters into a relational long format.
2. **Relational Joining & Feature Engineering**: Merged biological and environmental datasets by site and date, categorized thermal stress levels (>28.5 °C threshold), and reordered factors based on species sensitivity.
3. **Linear Mixed Modeling (LMM)**: Modeled bleaching percentage as a function of temperature, depth, and species (fixed effects), incorporating a nested random intercept structure `(1|Site) + (1|Site:Colony_ID)` to account for repeated measures on individual colonies within sampling sites.

# Key Findings & Visualizations

## 1. Species Susceptibility Across Thermal Stress Levels
*Description: Distribution and bootstrap confidence intervals of bleaching severity across species under Normal vs. High Thermal Stress (>28.5 °C).*

Finding: Elevated water temperatures trigger a sharp increase in bleaching response across all taxa. However, interspecific variation is significant: species such as *Acropora cervicornis* display extreme sensitivity under thermal stress, whereas species like *Porites lobata* demonstrate higher thermal tolerance.

## 2. Mixed-Effects Model Fit and Colony Dynamics
*Description: Observed bleaching percentage versus sea temperature across sampling sites, with colony-level predicted trajectories (`.fitted`) generated by the linear mixed model.*

Finding: Fitting a random-intercept model effectively isolates site-to-site baseline variation and individual colony heterogeneity while revealing a consistent positive relationship between temperature and bleaching severity across all sites.
